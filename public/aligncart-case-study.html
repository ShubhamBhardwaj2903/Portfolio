01 — Overview

What is
AlignCart?

AlignCart is a B2C mobile shopping app built for local retailers, shopkeepers, and small business owners across India. It gives them their own branded storefront where their customers can browse, order, and pay — without the retailer paying any platform commission or setup fee.

The app lives inside the AlignBooks ERP ecosystem. Products, inventory, orders, and billing are fully synced — so a shopkeeper managing their business in AlignBooks gets a customer-facing app at no extra cost.

No middlemen. No commission cuts like Blinkit or Swiggy Instamart. 100% of revenue goes directly to the seller.

02
02 — Problem

The gap quick commerce
left behind

Quick commerce platforms have made ordering groceries and daily essentials fast and convenient — but at a steep cost for the seller. Retailers list their products alongside hundreds of competitors, pay heavy commissions per order, and have zero ownership of the customer relationship.

Local shops — especially kirana stores, specialty retailers, and B2B suppliers — needed a way to reach their own customers digitally without giving up margin or brand identity.

Existing solutions either required expensive custom app development or locked sellers into generic marketplace experiences where their product gets buried alongside thousands of others.

  • Quick commerce platforms charge significant commissions per order, directly eating into retailer margins
  • Retailers have no ownership of customer data or relationships on third-party platforms
  • Products are competing for visibility on the same platform as thousands of other sellers
  • Custom app development was out of reach financially for most local businesses
  • WhatsApp ordering — the common workaround — is unscalable and hard to manage
03
03 — Research

Understanding
the users

AlignCart serves two distinct users: the vendor (the retailer who sets up and manages their store via AlignBooks) and the customer (who shops through the branded app). Research focused on both ends of this relationship.

Competitive analysis of Blinkit, Zepto, and JioMart revealed how heavy their interfaces are — optimised for browsing and impulse buying, not for repeat, relationship-based purchases from a known store.

"Customers of local stores valued familiarity over discovery — they knew what they wanted and from whom."

  • Shopkeepers using AlignBooks had no customer-facing channel — they relied on WhatsApp ordering, which was impossible to scale
  • Customers of local shops prioritise familiarity and trust over discovery — they shop from a store they already know
  • The app needed to feel like their shop's app, not a generic marketplace with competing products
  • Many target shopkeepers were not tech-savvy — every action needed to be obvious without needing explanation
  • Mid-range Android devices and variable connectivity made a lightweight, performant build a design priority

05
05 — Challenges

The problems
worth solving

01
Designing for Non-Tech Users
Shopkeepers range widely in digital literacy. Every action needed to be obvious without explanation — icons with labels, clear confirmation states, forgiving error handling, and zero ambiguous UI patterns throughout the product.
02
Fitting Features Without Clutter
Cart, coupons, delivery scheduling, address management, wallet, and payment — all within a clean, focused checkout flow. Information hierarchy was the core challenge: what do users need right now, and what can stay hidden until needed?
03
Lightweight & Performant
Many target users are on mid-range Android devices with variable connectivity. Design decisions had to actively support a lightweight build — thoughtful loading states, minimal heavy assets, and no features that would bloat the app size unnecessarily.
06
06 — Design Decisions

What we built
and why

OTP-First Login
Phone + OTP removes the password friction that causes drop-off among less tech-savvy users. One field, one action, in. The familiar pattern is trusted by millions of Indian users across UPI and banking apps.
Category-First Home
Rather than pushing algorithmically sorted products, the home screen leads with categories. Users of local shops know what they want — the design facilitates intent rather than manufacturing impulse.
Persistent Cart Bar
A sticky bottom bar showing item count and total throughout browsing reduces cognitive load and acts as a constant nudge toward checkout, without blocking product discovery.
Modular Checkout Flow
Coupons, delivery scheduling, and address selection are distinct, collapsible sections — not one overwhelming long form. Users can edit individual parts without losing their entire checkout state.
Built-in Wallet
A native wallet with transaction history gives repeat customers a faster payment path and builds loyalty without relying on third-party UPI apps for every transaction.
Unavailable Items Handling
Rather than silently blocking checkout, the app surfaces unavailable items clearly and prompts users to remove or replace — reducing frustration, abandonment, and support queries at scale.

10 — Reflections

What I
learnt

01
Speed Forces Clarity
A 1.5-month timeline for a 23-screen app left no room for indecision. Committing to a design direction early — and trusting it — was the only way to ship without cutting corners on quality.
02
Constraints Are Creative Fuel
Designing for low-powered devices and non-tech users pushed me toward cleaner, simpler patterns — which ultimately made the app better for everyone, not just the constrained users.
03
Know Your Two Users
AlignCart has a vendor and a customer. Keeping both mental models simultaneously prevented design decisions that optimised for one at the real expense of the other.
04
Systems Pay Off Fast
Building a component library in Figma upfront let me iterate quickly and gave developers a single source of truth — absolutely critical when working at this pace with a lean team.
