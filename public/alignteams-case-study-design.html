Overview

What is
AlignTeams?

AlignTeams is the people management module of the AlignBooks mobile app — a comprehensive ERP solution designed for Indian small and medium enterprises. Where AlignBooks handles accounting, GST compliance, and inventory, AlignTeams closes the loop on the human side of business operations.

The product enables business owners and managers to track employee attendance, manage field teams via geo-tracking, run payroll, and maintain HR records — all from a single mobile interface that mirrors the desktop ERP experience.

Discovery & Research

The Problem Space

AlignBooks had built a trusted desktop ERP for Indian SMEs. When the mobile app launched, it focused on accounting — invoices, GST, P&L on the go. But a critical gap emerged: business owners were still managing their teams entirely offline, using WhatsApp, paper attendance registers, and manual salary calculations.

This disconnect meant that even when financial data was digital, the people data driving that financial data remained analogue, error-prone, and invisible.

Research Methods

01Contextual
Interviews

Field Research with SME Owners

Conducted 12 in-depth interviews with business owners across manufacturing, retail, logistics, and services. Sessions were conducted in Hindi and English, with particular focus on the Tier 2 city context where AlignBooks has strong penetration.

02Competitive
Audit

Market Landscape Analysis

Analyzed 8 competing products including Keka, GreytHR, Zoho People, Darwinbox, and local SME-focused tools. Mapped feature parity, pricing models, and UX patterns to identify white space AlignTeams could own.

03Data
Analysis

AlignBooks Usage Analytics

Reviewed in-app analytics to understand which accounting features were co-used with payroll exports — identifying the 38% of users manually bridging HR data into their accounting entries. These became our early adopter cohort.

04Survey
Research

Quantitative Validation

Deployed a 14-question survey to 340 active AlignBooks users. 71% reported using a separate tool or manual method for attendance tracking. 58% said they would pay for integrated HR if it didn't require leaving their accounting app.

Key Research Insights

What we heard from users

  • Attendance is still tracked on paper or WhatsApp in 67% of businesses with fewer than 50 employees
  • Salary calculation takes 2–4 days per month for businesses with 10–30 employees
  • Field staff management (check-ins, routes, task allocation) has zero digital solution in this segment
  • Business owners distrust complex HRMS — they want "simple like WhatsApp, not complex like SAP"
  • Payroll errors are a top-3 cause of employee conflicts in the SMEs surveyed
  • Most users access AlignBooks 2–3x daily on mobile; desktop usage is secondary

Product Strategy

Defining the
Right Product

With research insights in hand, the core challenge was scoping: HR is a vast domain. Building everything would take years and deliver poor quality. The strategic decision was to go narrow and deep on the three pain points with the highest frequency and emotional weight in our research.

Strategic Pillars

📍

Pillar 1 — Presence

Know where your team is. Geo-tagged check-ins, field visit tracking, and live location — replacing WhatsApp pings with structured accountability.

📋

Pillar 2 — Attendance

Digital attendance with leave management, shift scheduling, and holiday calendars. One source of truth that feeds directly into payroll.

💰

Pillar 3 — Payroll

Auto-calculated salary slips with deductions, advances, bonuses, and PF/ESI compliance — with a one-tap journal entry push to AlignBooks.

Design Process

Designing for
Real Humans

The AlignBooks mobile app had an existing design language — functional but utilitarian. AlignTeams needed to feel like a natural extension while bringing a warmer, more human quality appropriate for a product that involves people, not just numbers.

Design Principles

01

Invisible Complexity

Payroll and compliance rules are deeply complex. The UI must absorb that complexity so the user sees simplicity. Every calculation should happen behind the scenes; every screen should ask only one question.

02

Familiar Patterns

Indian SME owners are comfortable with WhatsApp, phone calls, and paper. Our interface borrows familiar metaphors — the register, the check mark, the slip — and makes them digital without making them alien.

03

Trust Through Transparency

When we show a salary calculation, we show every line item. When we track location, we tell the employee why. Trust is earned by showing your work, not hiding it inside a number.

04

Speed Over Features

A business owner marking attendance during morning stand-up needs 3 taps, not 7. We ruthlessly traded feature richness for task speed in every primary flow.

Information Architecture

AlignTeams is accessed from the AlignBooks app's main navigation as a dedicated module. The IA reflects two distinct roles: the Manager View (owner / HR) and the Employee View — each surfacing only what's relevant to that person.

Core Navigation — Manager View

  • Dashboard: Today's attendance summary, pending approvals, quick-mark shortcuts
  • Team: Employee directory, profiles, documents, org chart
  • Attendance: Monthly calendar view, bulk mark, leave management, holidays
  • Field Tracking: Live map, visit history, route replay, check-in logs
  • Payroll: Monthly processing, salary structures, advances, payslip generation
  • Reports: Attendance reports, payroll summary, compliance (PF/ESI) exports

Core Navigation — Employee View

  • My Today: Mark attendance, check-in to field visit, today's tasks
  • My Attendance: Personal calendar, leave balance, leave application
  • My Payslips: Month-wise salary slips, download PDF, salary breakdown
  • My Documents: Offer letter, ID proofs, certificates uploaded by HR

Key Design Decisions

D1Attendance
UX

Bulk Attendance with Smart Defaults

Rather than marking each employee one-by-one, we defaulted to "All Present" and made exceptions the interaction — matching the mental model of a manager who assumes presence unless told otherwise. A single swipe-down marks everyone present. Testing showed this reduced marking time from 4.2 minutes to 47 seconds for a 20-person team.

D2Geo
Check-in

Location Without Surveillance Feel

Early prototypes felt invasive — constant tracking with a map visible to managers at all times. User testing with employees revealed discomfort and distrust. We pivoted to event-based geo-tagging: location is captured only at check-in and check-out, not continuously. Employees see exactly what's captured, addressing both the privacy concern and battery drain.

D3Payroll
Flow

The 4-Step Payroll Process

Payroll processing was redesigned as a linear, wizard-style flow: Review Attendance → Apply Adjustments → Verify Calculations → Approve and Push to Books. Each stage is a single screen with a single CTA. The wizard can be paused and resumed, addressing the finding that business owners often process payroll across multiple sittings.

D4Payslip
Design

Professional Payslips That Build Trust

Payslips were designed as shareable PDF documents matching professionally generated salary slips — with company letterhead, stamp-ready format, and itemized deductions. This became a high-signal trust feature: employees who receive a professional payslip via WhatsApp perceive the company as more organized and trustworthy.

Component Decisions

Component Design Decision Rationale
Attendance Status Pills Color + icon + text (triple coding) Accessibility for color-blind users; glanceability at distance
Employee Cards Photo optional, initials fallback Many SMEs don't have employee photos; avoid broken UI states
Salary Slider Removed — input field only Salary is precise; sliders invite errors and distrust
Leave Calendar Color-coded by leave type Managers pattern-match visually; color encodes faster than text
Map Check-in Card Static map thumbnail, not live Reduces loading time; live map unnecessary for location verification

Accessibility & Localization

The app was designed with Hindi language support from day one — not as an afterthought. String length variance between Hindi and English was accommodated in all UI components. Touch targets were set to a minimum of 48×48dp throughout, critical given the keyboard-and-stylus context many field supervisors operate in.

Roadmap Philosophy

Prioritization Framework

Features were prioritized using a custom ICE × User Pain matrix: each feature was scored on Impact (how many users benefit, how deeply), Confidence (validation evidence from research), and Effort (engineering complexity in sprint points). This was overlaid with a strategic importance filter — features that reinforced the payroll-to-books integration were given a 1.5x multiplier regardless of standalone score.

What Worked

Design & Product Decisions That Delivered

  • The "All Present by default" attendance model was the highest-praised UX decision in post-launch interviews
  • Event-based (not continuous) geo-tracking resolved employee privacy concerns without losing manager visibility
  • The 4-step payroll wizard reduced support tickets by 60% compared to the previous manual flow
  • Hindi-first UI strings drove significantly higher engagement in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities
  • Professional payslip PDFs became a viral feature — users shared them as proof of company legitimacy in hiring

What We'd Do Differently

Honest Retrospective

  • We underestimated the diversity of salary structures — more templates upfront would have reduced setup abandonment
  • Employee onboarding into the app needed a dedicated flow; managers struggled to get staff to self-register
  • Offline mode for attendance marking was deprioritized but proved critical in factory environments with poor connectivity
  • The map component was over-engineered in Phase 1 — a simpler address tag would have shipped faster and served 80% of needs
  • In-app compliance guidance (PF/ESI thresholds) needed legal review earlier; this delayed the payroll launch by 3 weeks

Design Learnings

Designing for Indian SME operators required constant recalibration of assumptions baked in from enterprise UX practice. These users don't have 30 minutes to onboard — the product must be useful in the first session. They don't read tooltips or help docs — the UI must explain itself through progressive disclosure and contextual prompts. They are deeply skeptical of anything that feels complex — every added feature must justify its cognitive cost in the interface.

The most valuable design skill on this project wasn't visual craft — it was the discipline to remove. Every screen that shipped was a screen that survived three rounds of "what if we cut this?"
